Deleting a few hundred photos should show up as a few gigabytes. Usually it
shows up as almost nothing, and the reason is that you have deleted one of three
copies. More handset guides are in [Phones](/topics/phones/). The same mechanism
on a laptop is in
[deleting files did not free any disk space](/deleting-files-not-freeing-disk-space/),
and a cached answer that outlives the change you made is also what makes
[DNS propagation](/dns-propagation-explained/) confusing.

## The first copy goes to a holding album

Both platforms treat delete as a move. The photo leaves your library and joins a
recently deleted album, where it stays for a set period so that an accidental
deletion can be undone. While it sits there it is still a file on the phone and
still counted against your storage.

Nothing about the interface suggests this. The library count drops, the grid
gets shorter, and the storage figure stays where it was. Emptying that album is
what actually releases the space, and it is a separate action in a separate
screen.

## The second copy is the app's own cache

A photo app does not read your original file every time it draws a grid. It
keeps thumbnails, preview renders and sometimes a full-resolution cached copy,
because decoding a modern photo on demand is slow enough to feel broken.

That cache is sized against your library as it was, not as it is. After a large
deletion it is holding renders of photos that no longer exist, and it will not
necessarily prune itself the same day. Clearing it is safe. The cost is that the
first scroll afterwards is slower while the app rebuilds what it needs.

## The third copy belongs to the cloud

If iCloud Photos or Google Photos is on, the phone is showing you a view of a
library that lives elsewhere. Deleting locally queues a deletion upstream, and
until that round trip finishes the local file may still be held.

This is also where people lose photos they meant to keep. Sync is bidirectional.
A deletion you make to free space on the phone removes the photo from the backup
as well, which is the opposite of what most people assume a backup does.

<Callout type="danger" title="Clear your own copy first">
  Before deleting in bulk, get the photos off the phone and off the account, onto something
  you control. A deletion that syncs is not recoverable once the holding album expires.
</Callout>

If the handset feels slow as well as full, storage is only one of three causes:
see [why a phone slows down as it gets older](/phone-slows-down-gets-older/).

## What to expect once all three are clear

Storage figures on both platforms are estimates that settle over minutes, not
instantly. Expect the number to move in steps rather than at once, and expect a
category labelled as system or other to absorb some of what you freed, because
the phone will take a little of it back for its own caches.

The exact wording and position of these screens moves with each major OS
release, which is why this post describes the mechanism rather than a menu path.
A tested walkthrough with the current paths is coming.
