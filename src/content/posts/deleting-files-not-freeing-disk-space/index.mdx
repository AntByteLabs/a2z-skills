A file you deleted is still costing you space, and the number in the storage
panel has not moved. Several mechanisms can explain the difference. The cause depends on the
filesystem, the app and which storage figure you are reading. More desktop guides are in [Windows & Mac](/topics/windows-mac/); the same
problem on a handset is in
[phone storage full after deleting photos](/phone-storage-full-after-deleting-photos/),
and another storage question is
[why an Excel workbook can be larger than its visible data](/excel-file-size-too-large/).

<Callout type="note" title="Correction, 27 October 2026">
  The earlier recovery answer implied deleted bytes remain recoverable until overwritten.
  Recoverability depends on the storage and filesystem; emptying the bin is not a safe way
  to preserve a file for later recovery.
</Callout>

## Removing a name and releasing storage are different events

Free space depends on the allocations that remain on the volume. Moving a file
to a bin can retain its allocation. A snapshot or another retained copy can also
preserve content after it disappears from the current folder.

Deletion is not a recovery guarantee. The filesystem, SSD trimming, encryption
and subsequent activity can affect whether old content remains accessible.
[Microsoft's recovery guidance](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/backup-recovery/windows-file-recovery)
explains why recovery cannot be assumed. A separate backup is a different
protection from hoping deleted data can be reconstructed.

## Four things that keep claiming the space

**The Recycle Bin or Trash.** Deleting moves the file rather than releasing it.
The space is still allocated and still counted. This is the one most people
check, and it is the reason the other three get missed.

**Snapshots and local accounting.** A snapshot can preserve data after a file
changes or disappears from the current view. Apple explains that Time Machine
local snapshots are managed automatically and their space is counted as
available storage. A large snapshot figure alone does not prove that macOS
cannot make room for new work. Windows backup and restore features have
different storage arrangements; File History is not interchangeable with
Time Machine.

**A program still holding a file open.** Handle and sharing rules can delay
deletion or prevent it, depending on the operating system and operation.
Microsoft documents deletion waiting for handles to close. Ending the relevant
process can release its handles, but that does not remove snapshots or other
retained copies.

**Cloud and local copies.** Sync services can keep downloaded files, offline
copies and separate deleted-items areas. Their policies differ. A cloud
account's usage is a different measurement from free space on the computer,
so compare the relevant local figure before attributing the change to sync.

A related case is an update that will not finish until you restart, explained in
[why Windows has to restart to finish an update](/windows-restart-finish-installing-updates/).

## How to tell which one is holding your space

Start by identifying the volume and category that still consumes the space.
The bin, app downloads and backup storage are different places to investigate.
A restart can release a process's open files, but a persistent problem after a
restart does not establish that snapshots caused it. Microsoft's storage
guidance covers other categories, including system files and locally downloaded
cloud content.

<Callout type="warning" title="Before you delete a snapshot">
  Removing a snapshot removes that restore point. Other backups may exist, but deleting
  backup history is a different decision from removing a temporary download. Apple manages
  Time Machine local snapshots automatically and counts their space as available.
</Callout>

The exact panel that reports storage, and the wording beside it, moves between
Windows builds and macOS releases, so this post explains the mechanisms. The vendor sources below provide their
current instructions; this page does not claim a device-specific repair test.
