Ask the same question twice and you get two answers. People read that as the
tool being unreliable, when it is closer to the opposite: the variation is
deliberate, and three of the four causes have nothing to do with chance. More
tool write-ups are in [AI Tools](/topics/ai-tools/). The same measurement
confusion from the other direction is in
[why AI detectors flag human writing](/ai-detectors-flagging-human-writing/),
and for systems that look consistent while being wrong, read
[why automations fail silently](/automations-failing-silently/).

## Each word is picked from a shortlist

A language model does not retrieve an answer. It predicts one word at a time,
producing a ranked list of candidates with a probability attached to each, then
picking one. If it always took the top candidate, its writing would be flat and
repetitive, so by default it samples from the shortlist instead.

Settings called temperature and top-p control how adventurous that pick is. A
low temperature keeps it close to the most likely word; a higher one lets it
reach further down the list. Either way, one different word early on changes
what the model is continuing from, so the two answers diverge from that point
and keep diverging.

## Even the settings that should be deterministic are not quite

Turn the temperature to zero through the API and the model takes the top
candidate every time, which ought to be repeatable. In practice it usually is
not, and the reason is arithmetic rather than design.

Floating point addition is not associative: adding the same numbers in a
different order can give a very slightly different result. On a GPU the work is
split across many parallel units, and the order in which their results are
combined depends on how the batch was arranged, which depends on who else is
using the server. Occasionally a probability shifts enough to change which
candidate ranks first, and from there the answers separate.

<Callout type="note" title="This is why a seed is not a guarantee">
  A fixed seed removes the randomness in the sampling. It does not remove the variation in
  how the arithmetic was batched, so reproducibility is close rather than exact.
</Callout>

## The conversation is part of the question

Each reply is produced by reading the entire thread, not just your last message.
The same question asked at the start of a chat and ten exchanges later is two
different inputs, because the second one carries everything in between.

Saved memories work the same way across sessions. Two people asking an identical
question get different answers because the model is reading different context,
not because it changed its mind.

## The wrapper and the model both change

Every product wraps your prompt in hidden instructions about tone, format and
what to refuse. A custom assistant, a company deployment and the public website
all use different ones, so the same typed words arrive as different inputs.

Underneath, the model itself is updated. An answer you got last month may be
unreproducible today for the plain reason that the thing answering is not the
same version. If an exact output matters, save it when you get it.
