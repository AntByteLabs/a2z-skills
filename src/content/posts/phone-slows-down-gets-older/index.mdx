A phone that felt instant in its first year feels tired in its third, and the
processor in it is exactly the same one. More handset guides are in
[Phones](/topics/phones/). The storage half of this is covered in
[phone storage still full after deleting photos](/phone-storage-full-after-deleting-photos/),
and the desktop version of the same ageing question is in
[why Windows has to restart to finish an update](/windows-restart-finish-installing-updates/).

## The storage fills, and full storage is slow storage

Phones use flash memory, which cannot overwrite a block in place. To write to a
block that holds old data, the controller has to erase it first, and erasing
happens in larger units than writing. While there is plenty of free space, the
controller writes to blocks that are already clear and tidies up later, when the
phone is idle.

As the drive fills, that spare room disappears. The controller starts having to
clear space before it can write, in the middle of whatever you are doing. Storage pressure can therefore contribute to a slowdown, but a percentage
alone does not diagnose it. The effect depends on the device and workload; a
slow network or a particular app can also be responsible.

## The battery ages, and the phone protects itself

A lithium battery loses the ability to deliver peak current long before it loses
the ability to hold charge. When a processor suddenly demands a burst of power,
an aged battery's voltage sags, and if it sags far enough the phone shuts down
rather than corrupt anything.

To avoid that, phones reduce peak performance as the battery degrades. Apple
added this after a run of unexpected shutdowns, disclosed it following
considerable criticism for not saying so earlier, and now reports battery health
and documents how performance management depends on battery condition and
other factors. Android devices have manufacturer-specific policies; the same
behaviour should not be assumed for every handset.

<Callout type="tip" title="Check which one you have">
  Battery health is reported in settings on both platforms, and so is free storage. Reading
  those two numbers takes a minute and tells you whether to spend money on a battery or an
  afternoon deleting things.
</Callout>

## The software got heavier and the hardware did not

Each OS release is built against the phones being sold that year. Features are
added, background services multiply, and the apps you use ship bigger updates
targeting newer devices. None of it is dramatic on its own. Three years of it
compounds.

This is the part you cannot undo, short of staying on an old release, which
costs you security updates and is a bad trade.

## What to do about it

Free storage first, because it is free, reversible and frequently the whole
problem. Check battery health second, because a replacement may cost less than a
new phone, but its benefit depends on what caused the slowdown. Treat the software weight as the
reason to eventually replace the handset, rather than as something to fight.
