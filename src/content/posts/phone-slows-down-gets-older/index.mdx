A phone that once felt quick can become frustrating to use. That does not
identify one worn-out component. The delay may come from the phone, an app or
a network request that has not finished. More handset explanations are in
[Phones](/topics/phones/).

<Callout type="note" title="Correction, 27 October 2026">
  The earlier summary generalised battery throttling across phones and promised that two
  causes could be reversed in an afternoon. Battery behaviour depends on the model, and the
  time or benefit of a repair cannot be promised without diagnosing the device.
</Callout>

## Storage pressure can contribute

A phone needs local space for apps and their working data. A full device can
therefore behave differently from one with room available. The amount of space
needed depends on the device and workload; a percentage alone cannot prove why
an app is slow.

[Apple's slow-device guidance](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/102598)
identifies storage as one possible contributor. It also covers network delays,
temperature, power settings and unresponsive apps. A handset that pauses only
while loading a web page needs a different investigation from one that is slow
with local content as well.

Storage warnings also need a location. [Deleting photos](/phone-storage-full-after-deleting-photos/)
may affect the online library, local copies or both. Buying cloud capacity does
not add physical storage to a phone.

## Battery-related limits depend on the model

Apple's [iPhone battery and performance documentation](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/101575)
explains how an ageing battery can become less able to supply peak power.
Performance management depends on conditions including battery state and
temperature. This is an iPhone-specific explanation with model differences,
not evidence that every old Android phone uses the same policy.

A replacement battery can help when battery condition is responsible for a
limitation. It cannot establish that a separate network delay or faulty app has
been repaired. The device's own battery information and manufacturer guidance
are more useful than inferring the cause from its age alone.

## Apps and software can change the workload

The processor is not upgraded when an app gains new features. A different
software workload can make the same hardware feel less responsive, but it is
too broad to say that every operating-system update makes every phone slower.
An individual app problem can also coincide with an update without proving that
the whole operating system is responsible.

[Google's slow-Android guidance](https://support.google.com/android/answer/7667018?hl=en)
separates connection problems, device storage and app troubleshooting. It notes
that settings and supported steps vary by device. Android is not one uniform
handset with a single battery-health screen or repair path.

## A reset has costs and limits

A factory reset removes device data. It may address some software conditions,
but it does not replace a worn battery, enlarge storage or guarantee that the
problem will stay away after apps return. It should not be described as a
routine reversible clean-up.

Google includes backup and restoration considerations in its supported Android
guidance. A device-specific repair guide would also need the actual model,
software version and observed result. This explanation does not claim a reset
or battery-replacement test.

The same caution applies on a desktop: [Windows update restarts](/windows-restart-finish-installing-updates/)
have several stages, so one slow restart is not by itself evidence of failing
hardware.
