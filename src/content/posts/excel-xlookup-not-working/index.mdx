Four things cause almost every XLOOKUP failure. Check them in this order,
because the first two take ten seconds each and the last one takes a minute.

## Check which error you are getting

The error tells you which half of the problem you have. `#NAME?` is about your
Excel; `#N/A` is about your data.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Read the error, not the formula">
    Click the cell and look at the error itself. `#NAME?` means Excel does not recognise the
    function. `#N/A` means it ran and found nothing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm your Excel version">
    Go to **File → Account → About Excel**. XLOOKUP needs Microsoft 365 or Excel 2021. On
    anything older, the function does not exist.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Compare the data types">
    Put `=ISTEXT(A2)` beside your lookup value and `=ISTEXT(D2)` beside the first cell of
    your lookup array. If one says TRUE and the other FALSE, that is your answer.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Fix a type mismatch

Numbers stored as text are the most common cause. Select the column, open
**Data → Text to Columns**, and click Finish without changing anything: Excel
re-reads the column and converts what it can.

<Callout type="tip">
  Wrap the lookup value in `VALUE()` as a quick test. If the formula starts working, the
  column was text and needs converting properly.
</Callout>

## Fix a range that moved

If the formula worked yesterday and not today, a row insert may have shifted the
lookup array. Turn the range into a table with **Ctrl+T** and refer to the table
columns by name; the reference then follows the data.

<WhatBroke>
  On the test machine, Text to Columns converted the column but left two cells with a
  trailing non-breaking space, which still failed to match. `=CODE(RIGHT(A2,1))` returned
  160 rather than 32, which is the giveaway. `TRIM()` does not remove character 160;
  `SUBSTITUTE(A2, CHAR(160), "")` does.
</WhatBroke>
