An AI detector tells you a number. It does not tell you who wrote the text, and
the gap between those two things is where the damage happens. More tool
write-ups are in [AI Tools](/topics/ai-tools/). For other software that guesses
your intent and gets it wrong, read
[Excel changing numbers into dates](/excel-changing-numbers-into-dates/) and
[why automations fail silently](/automations-failing-silently/).

## What the score actually measures

A language model works by predicting the next word. Any text can therefore be
scored by how closely it matches what a model would have predicted. Text that
follows the expected path scores as predictable. Text that takes unusual turns,
changes rhythm or uses an odd word scores as unpredictable.

Detectors are built on that signal, often combined with how much the sentence
lengths and structures vary across a document. The output is a claim about the
statistical shape of the writing. It is then presented as a claim about its
origin, which is a different claim and does not follow from the first.

## Why good writing fails

Clear prose is predictable prose. Short sentences, plain words, one idea at a
time, consistent structure: every habit that makes writing easier to read also
makes the next word easier to guess. Editing pushes a draft further in that
direction, so a piece that has been through several passes scores worse than the
messy first version it came from.

The same effect hits anyone writing in a second language. A smaller working
vocabulary and more conventional sentence patterns produce exactly the profile
the detectors treat as machine-like, and this bias has been reported repeatedly
in published evaluations.

<Callout type="warning" title="The base rate makes it worse">
  Even a detector that is right most of the time produces more false accusations than true
  ones when most of the work it scans is human. Accuracy on a balanced test set says nothing
  useful about a class where nearly everyone wrote their own essay.
</Callout>

## Why the vendors cannot fix it

The signal degrades as models improve. A model that writes less predictably is
harder to distinguish from a person who writes unpredictably, and the whole
method depends on that distinction holding. OpenAI withdrew its own classifier
for exactly this reason, citing accuracy too low to be useful.

There is also no stable target. Detectors are tuned against the models that
exist now, and are then run against text produced by models released since,
edited by people, translated, or passed through a grammar tool. Each of those
changes the statistics the score depends on.

## What to do if you are on the receiving end

Keep the evidence that a score cannot produce. Document history showing the
piece being written, with revisions and dead ends, is the thing detectors cannot
fake and cannot argue with. Save it before you submit, not after you are
accused.

If you are the one deciding, treat a score as a reason to read the work and ask
the writer about it, never as a finding. A number with no published error rate,
applied to a population that is overwhelmingly innocent, is not evidence of
anything.
